Your first year is all about building a foundation. Before you can learn how to treat an illness, you have to master how a healthy body functions.

Anatomy & Physiology (A&P) +

The structural map of the human body and how its organ systems interact.

Lecture 1: Homeostasis & Cellular Transport
  • Homeostasis: Maintenance of a stable internal environment via negative feedback loops (e.g., body temperature, blood glucose regulation).
  • Cell Membrane: Semi-permeable lipid bilayer. Passive transport (diffusion, osmosis) moves down gradient; active transport (Sodium-Potassium pump) requires ATP energy.
Lecture 2: Major Organ Systems Overview
  • Cardiovascular & Respiratory: Combined gas exchange and transport mechanism distributing oxygenated blood and removing metabolic waste products.
Biochemistry +

Essential chemical principles behind human metabolism, genetics, and cellular processes.

Lecture 1: Macromolecules & Cellular Energy Mechanics
  • Carbohydrate Pathways: Glycolysis (cytosol, anaerobic, net 2 ATP output) vs. Krebs Cycle & Electron Transport Chain (mitochondria, aerobic, generates 32-36 ATP).
Theoretical Foundations of Nursing +

Introducing the history, nursing pioneers, and frameworks that define patient care.

Lecture 1: Core Metaparadigm & Pioneer Models
  • Nursing Metaparadigm: Consists of 4 pillars: Person, Environment, Health, and Nursing.
  • Florence Nightingale: Environmental Theory emphasizes optimizing clean air, pure water, ventilation, light, and hygiene.
Health Assessment +

Learning to interview patients and physically examine them.

Lecture 1: The IPPA Framework & Vitals
  • Standard Physical Exam Order: Inspection, Palpation, Percussion, Auscultation.
  • Abdominal Exception: Inspection, Auscultation, Percussion, Palpation to prevent altering natural bowel frequencies.
Fundamentals of Nursing Practice +

Infection control, vital signs, bed-making, and basic patient safety guidelines.

Lecture 1: Infection Control & The Nursing Process
  • ADPIE Framework: Assessment, Diagnosis, Planning, Implementation, Evaluation.
  • Isolation Precautions: Airborne (N95), Droplet (Surgical mask), Contact (Gowns and gloves).
Microbiology & Parasitology +

The study of bacteria, viruses, and parasites, and how they cause infectious diseases.

Lecture 1: Bacterial Taxonomy & Gram Staining
  • Gram-Positive: Stains purple (thick peptidoglycan layer).
  • Gram-Negative: Stains pink/red (thin peptidoglycan with lipopolysaccharide outer wall containing endotoxins).
Pharmacology +

How drugs work in the body, calculating safe medication dosages, and monitoring side effects.

Lecture 1: Mechanism of Actions & Safe Administration
  • Pharmacokinetics vs. Dynamics: Kinetics involves body actions on a drug (ADME); Dynamics tracks drug actions on the biological system.
  • The Rights of Medication Administration: Checking Right Patient, Right Drug, Right Dose, Right Route, Right Time, and proper documentation.
Nutrition & Diet Therapy +

Dietary planning for both health maintenance and specific disease management.

Lecture 1: Therapeutic Modification Strategies
  • Renal Restrictions: Restricting Potassium, Sodium, Phosphorus, and fluid loading to reduce pressure on the kidneys.
Community Health Nursing 1 +

Transitioning focus from individual patients to treating families and understanding local public health.

Lecture 1: Levels of Public Health Prevention
  • Primary: Disease avoidance (Vaccines). Secondary: Early detection (Screenings). Tertiary: Rehabilitation (Physical Therapy).
Care of Mother, Child, and Adolescent +

Pregnancy, labor, delivery, postpartum care, newborn assessment, and pediatric milestone metrics.

Lecture 1: Obstetric Milestones & APGAR Scoring
  • APGAR Matrix: Evaluates Appearance, Pulse, Grimace, Activity, and Respiration at 1 and 5 minutes post-delivery.
Medical-Surgical Nursing (Care of Clients) +

A massive core topic mapping out complex adult illnesses—cellular, fluid, or metabolic alterations.

Lecture 1: Fluid, Electrolyte & Metabolic Disturbances
  • Potassium Safety: Normal values range from 3.5 to 5.0 mEq/L. Never give Potassium via IV push.
  • DKA vs. HHS: DKA happens primarily in Type 1 DM (ketones present, acidosis); HHS happens in Type 2 DM (extreme hyperglycemia, no ketones).
Mental Health / Psychiatric Nursing +

Assessing and caring for patients facing maladaptive behaviors, acute psychiatric issues, depression, and anxiety.

Lecture 1: Communication Boundaries & Dynamics
  • Transference vs. Countertransference: Transference is patient projecting onto the nurse; countertransference is nurse projecting onto the patient.
Care of the Older Person (Geriatrics) +

Managing the multi-layered healthcare needs, cognitive changes, and chronic illnesses of elderly populations.

Lecture 1: Differentiating Delirium vs. Dementia
  • Delirium: Acute, abrupt, temporary confusion window caused by secondary vectors (UTI, dehydration). Dementia: Progressive, slow, and irreversible cognitive decline.
Nursing Research (1 and 2) +

Learning how to critique medical literature, design a study, use biostatistics, and draft a thesis statement.

Lecture 1: Methodologies & Ethical Compliance
  • Quantitative vs. Qualitative: Quantitative uses data values and numbers; qualitative gathers live, narrative patient perspectives and experiences.
Critical Care & Disaster Nursing +

Managing life-threatening conditions inside ICU environments and handling public health triage chains.

Lecture 1: Mass Casualty START Triage Protocol
  • Color Coding: RED: Immediate life threat. YELLOW: Delayed, stable trauma. GREEN: Walking wounded. BLACK: Deceased/Pulseless.
Nursing Leadership and Management +

Learning hospital organizational structures, legal responsibilities, scheduling, and ethics.

Lecture 1: Delegation Mechanics & Legal Boundaries
  • The EAT Delegation Principle: RNs cannot delegate items requiring Evaluation, Assessment, or Teaching to assistive personnel.
Intensive Nursing Practicum +

Transitioning to full clinical shifts under a preceptor, handling a multi-patient load to build autonomy.

Lecture 1: Professional Transitions & SBAR Communication
  • SBAR Standardization: Efficient clinical updates structure using Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation.
Competency Appraisal +

Structured review classes designed to synthesize all four years of study for board exams.

Lecture 1: High-Yield Test Strategies
  • Priority Principle: Always choose acute/sudden physiological dynamic threats over chronic, expected symptomatic presentations.

Our Purpose

Nursing school presents an incredibly dense volume of medical literature and practical frameworks. NursingNotes was engineered to bridge the massive gap between complex textbook theory and fast-paced clinical environments. By distilling extensive lectures into clean, structural insights, this hub functions as an efficient cognitive support space for future medical professionals.
